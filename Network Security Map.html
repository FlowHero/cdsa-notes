Cross-Cutting Detection Themes
01
Spoofing buys stealth but causes blindness
Forging a source IP means replies go elsewhere — so recon/hijack spoofing is almost always paired with ARP poisoning/MitM to recover responses. That pairing is usually what exposes the attack.
02
Bind IP ↔ MAC ↔ behaviour ↔ timing
Signatures get evaded (fragmentation, TTL, decoys); the mismatches don't — IP/MAC inconsistency, trusted source at the wrong time, a device acting unlike its baseline. Strongest detection lever.
03
Make your device act like the destination host
Delayed reassembly (fragmentation), TTL normalization (TTL evasion), full session reconstruction (decoy/overlap) — all collapse the sensor-vs-host gap that evasion exploits.
04
Crafted traffic is uniform; real traffic is messy
Identical lengths, non-random base ports, unsolicited replies, implausible TTLs, illegal flag combos — uniformity and impossibility are the tells. Real traffic has natural variation.
05
Unauthenticated protocols are the root cause
ARP (no auth) · 802.11 management frames (unauthenticated on WPA2) · IP source field (no integrity check). Modern fixes authenticate or validate: DAI (Dynamic ARP Inspection) · 802.11w / WPA3 (PMF) · BCP 38 ingress filtering (IP spoofing). The pattern: bolt-on fixes for protocols designed without adversaries in mind.
